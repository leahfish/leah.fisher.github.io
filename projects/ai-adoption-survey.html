AI Adoption & Reporting Survey Design

Penn Quarter Partners · Healthcare Policy & Research Intern · May 2025 - Aug 2025

Situation

Leadership at Penn Quarter Partners wanted to establish firm-wide guidelines for AI use and decide which platforms the firm would formally support. Making those decisions well required knowing how staff were actually using AI in their day-to-day work, and that information didn't exist in any organized form. What leadership had instead was anecdote: scattered impressions of who was using what, with no way to tell whether a tool mentioned in a meeting reflected genuine adoption or one person's experiment..

Task

Working with the firm's internal AI working group, I was asked to design an instrument that would capture honest, comparable data on AI use across teams and seniority levels, in a format that could actually inform policy rather than just confirm what people already assumed.

Action

I built a survey in Google Forms covering which tools staff were using, the specific tasks they applied them to, and where they had run into limitations or stopped using something. The design choice that mattered most was structuring questions so responses could be compared across teams rather than collected as open-ended anecdotes. I also kept the instrument short enough that busy staff would finish it, on the reasoning that a complete picture from a high response rate is worth more than a detailed picture from a handful of people.

Result

The survey gave leadership a firm-wide, evidence-based view of AI adoption to replace assumptions, providing the input needed for decisions about guidelines and platform support.
